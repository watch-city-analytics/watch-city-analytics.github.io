The arrangement nobody designed but everybody accepted

One platform now sits between most programs and their own film. It handles exchange with opponents, it holds the archive, and it sells the breakdown back. Prices climb every year, the tiers are gated on how much of your own history you are allowed to keep, and programs that cannot pay fall behind programs that can.

Read the terms and it gets worse. Film uploaded by schools can be used to improve the platform's own analysis — the analysis it then provides to every other program, including the one you play on Friday. Your film helps train the system your opponent uses against you, and you pay a subscription for the privilege.

That is a monopoly quietly squeezing athletic departments and schools that have no room left in the budget. We think it can be done better.

You do not have to leave. You have to stop paying for the part you can own.

How this actually works

Nobody cancels anything. There is no migration, no fight with your league, and no week where your staff cannot exchange film.

i

Keep the platform for what only it can do

Exchange with opponents and recruiting profiles are network effects. No one can replicate them and we will not pretend otherwise. Those stay exactly where they are.

ii

Move your archive somewhere you control

This is the step that makes the rest possible, because the expensive tiers are mostly buying you storage. Your film goes into storage your organization owns, at a cost that does not climb because somebody repriced a plan.

iii

Drop to the tier that covers what is left

For most programs the premium tiers exist to deliver breakdown and storage. Once those are handled, the entry tier does the remaining job. The difference is real money, and it is subtraction rather than a projection.

iv

Run the breakdown on your own stack

Your tagging scheme, your tendencies, the questions your staff actually asks. Built on AI that runs against your film, in your account, training nobody else's system.

The saving funds the work. What you get back is analysis your opponents do not have.

Why we care about this one

This is where the firm started. The original question was how a town that could not afford the subscription gives its kids the same shot as the town down the road.

Coaches at this level are volunteers or close to it. The Sunday spent cutting film is time taken from a family, and the programs that can buy their way out of it are not the ones that need the help. Giving that evening back to a youth coach is worth as much to us as anything else we do.
