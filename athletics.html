The arrangement nobody designed but everybody accepted

One platform now sits between most programs and their own film. It handles exchange with opponents, it holds the archive, and it sells the breakdown back. Its published tiers for a single high school program run $1,500, $2,500 and $4,000 a year. Moving up a tier buys you wider analysis and faster turnaround — and it roughly triples how many hours of your own history you are allowed to keep.

Districts buying for several schools at once pay a good deal more, and those figures sit in public board minutes if you want to go looking. We are not going to quote you somebody else’s renewal, because the only number that matters to you is on your own.

What the storage underneath actually costs. A season of game, scout and practice film for one high school football program is roughly seventy-five hours, or about 225 gigabytes. Held for a year in ordinary cloud storage, that costs somewhere between $19 and $58, depending on the provider and how much of it gets watched. Even 750 hours — the largest published tier’s allowance — costs under $600 a year to store at list rates.

That gap is not the scandal it looks like. Most of what you are paying for is the breakdown labor, the turnaround time, the apps and the exchange network, and all of those are real work that somebody has to do. The honest point is narrower and more useful: when a tier upgrade arrives in front of you as an hours limit, storage is not the thing you are being asked to buy.

You do not have to leave. You have to stop paying for the part you can own.

What the agreement says about your film

We went and read it, because almost nobody does. Everything below is from the published Organization Terms of Service dated February 9, 2026, cited by section so you can check every word of it against your own copy.

You own the film. The agreement says so plainly, and the base license the platform takes is narrow: it can use your material to the extent needed to provide you the service.

Two carve-outs widen that considerably, and both outlive your subscription. Practice video run through the platform’s own breakdown tools carries an irrevocable, sublicensable license, and a perpetual right to prepare derivative works from it, for the development and improvement of the company’s products (§4.2). Game video you put into the open exchange is added to the library available to that platform’s users, along with a perpetual right to derive and generate data from it (§5.2). Section 12.4 lists what survives termination, and both of those are on the list.

Ask what happens to your archive when you leave. On termination, §12.3 switches your access off. The list of surviving rights in §12.4 does not include anything that obliges the company to hand your material back to you, in any format, within any window. You can pull selected clips out as MP4 while you are still a customer, but the tagging and breakdown that make the archive worth having do not travel with them.

That is the published agreement, and it is not necessarily your agreement. Districts that sign their own data privacy addendum often get more — we have read one that obliges the vendor to help export all protected data back to a withdrawing district, with certified deletion afterward. If you have signed something like that, you have an export right the standard terms do not give you. It is worth finding out which of the two you are actually operating under before your next renewal, and your business office will have the file.

How this actually works

Nobody cancels anything. There is no migration, no fight with your league, and no week where your staff cannot exchange film.

i

Keep the platform for what only it can do

Opponent exchange and recruiting profiles run on network effects. Nobody can replicate those, and we will not pretend otherwise. Those stay exactly where they are.

ii

Move your archive somewhere you control

This is the step that makes the rest possible, because the hours limit is what moves you up a tier in the first place. Your film goes into storage your organization owns, at a cost that does not climb because somebody repriced a plan.

iii

Drop to the tier that covers what is left

For most programs the premium tiers exist to deliver breakdown and storage. Once those are handled, the entry tier does the remaining job. The difference is real money, and it is subtraction rather than a projection.

iv

Run the breakdown on your own stack

Your tagging scheme, your tendencies, the questions your staff actually asks. Built on AI that runs against your film, in your account, training nobody else's system.

The saving funds the work. What you get back is analysis your opponents do not have.
