The discipline of a turnaround, without the extraction.

We are building a firm around two kinds of people who are rarely in the same room.

What we actually do, described honestly

When private equity takes over a company, the first thing it sends in is operators. People who understand how the work is really done, who find where effort is being wasted, and who rebuild the process around what matters. That part works. It is why the model exists.

What happens afterward is the part people object to. The gains are extracted, the balance sheet carries the cost, and the operation is left thinner than it was found.

We do the first half, for organizations nobody is buying. Schools, towns, family businesses, athletic programs, independent studios — operations run by people who are doing too much with too little and have never had access to that kind of attention. We find the waste, rebuild the process, and leave the gains where they were made.

The same discipline. The opposite purpose.

That is the work, and it is why the hiring looks the way it does.

HOW WE WORKSmall engagements, scoped to one workflow at a time. Remote, with real time on site when the work needs eyes on it. Based in Maine and Massachusetts.
WHAT WE WILL NOT DOHeadcount reduction as the goal. We are hired to give capacity back to people who are stretched, not to justify removing them.

Two kinds of people, deliberately

Most firms in this space hire one or the other and hope the gap closes itself. It does not. The whole method depends on both being in the room.

Industry veterans

You have run something. Operations, finance, athletics, property management, a municipal department, a studio. You know which step of your process is load-bearing, which number is implausible on sight, and which stakeholder quietly kills projects in month three.

You do not need to know AI. We will teach you, and you will be better at this than people who came the other way, because the judgment is the hard part and you already have it.

New graduates who are fluent

You came up with these tools in your hands. You do not find them remarkable, which is exactly right — you reach for them the way an earlier generation reached for a spreadsheet, and you can tell quickly when a model is confidently wrong.

What you will get here is the thing a first job rarely offers: sitting beside someone who has run a real operation for twenty years while they explain why the obvious automation would break the business.

Between them sit our architects — people who have designed production data systems at enterprise scale, and who know what it costs to get your data back out of somewhere you should not have put it.

What the job is really like

i

You will spend more time asking than building

Most of this work is understanding a process that nobody has ever written down, including exceptions the person describing it has stopped noticing. The building is the short part.

ii

You will be expected to say no

We tell clients when a workflow is not worth automating. Anyone who cannot deliver that message will struggle here, because it is most of what we are actually selling.

iii

The clients are not faceless

A school district. A town office. A family that has run a building for forty years. You will meet the people whose week you are changing, and you will hear about it if you get it wrong.

iv

Nothing you build should be hard to take away from us

We hand over systems that run on the client's own accounts, documented well enough that they could replace us. That is a constraint on how you build, and it is not negotiable.

Get in touch before we post a role

We are growing deliberately, which means the best time to talk to us is before there is a job listing. Tell us what you have run, or what you can build, and what kind of problem you want to spend your time on.

Write to jobs@watchcityanalytics.com. A few paragraphs in your own words is worth more to us than a formatted CV, though send that too if you have one.

If you are a veteran of an industry and assume you are too late to work in AI: you are the person this page is written for.
