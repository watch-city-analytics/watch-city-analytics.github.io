It started with one career. It runs on the people that career introduced.

Large companies have architects. People whose whole job is to ask what happens to the data, what this costs in year three, and what it takes to leave a vendor. Everyone else gets sold to as though they have those people, and they do not.

This practice began with Sanjay Patel’s thirty years building IT systems, nearly three decades of it at EMC and Dell, doing one thing over and over — working out what an organization actually needs, then deciding what should be built and, as often, what should not. That is a long time to learn how organizations get trapped by their own tools.

It is also a long time to meet people. Operators who ran finance, athletics, property and production before they ever touched AI. Architects who have built the systems that hold the records of very large companies. Builders, designers and developers who are the best in the room at their own trade. Not strangers from a directory — people he has worked beside, over decades, and would put his name next to.

The arrangement is fractional. You will never afford these people full time, and you do not need them full time. You need the right one of them for the few weeks your problem actually takes. That is what this is: senior specialists, for the fraction of time the job calls for, and not an hour more.

What makes it a practice rather than a list of contractors is the method, and the method is his. Start with one job. Watch it actually get done, because what a process is supposed to be and what it really is are never quite the same. Measure what it costs in hours before anybody proposes anything. Build on what you already own, so you can leave. Everyone who works here works that way, or they do not work here.

It started with a town whose youth sports teams could not afford to compete

A few years ago the question was narrow and practical: youth sports run on video now, and the software that makes that work costs more than some towns can pay. How does a community that cannot afford it give its kids the same shot as the town down the road?

At the time, it could not. Coaches, most of them volunteers, spend their week watching video — their own games, and the team they play next — looking for the patterns that decide the next one. That work sat behind a subscription some programs could absorb and others could not, and the gap compounded every season. Then it became clear what was possible once AI was applied to the problem — not as a product to sell, but as a way to rebuild the capability out of parts a town could actually afford.

That is the whole firm. Build what a small organization is being priced out of, on infrastructure it controls.

We are small on purpose, and specialized on purpose.

Not a generalist firm that will take any job. A short list of worlds we actually understand, because somebody here has done the work or sat beside the person doing it. When we do not know a trade, we say so and bring in somebody who does, by name.

Government and public bodies

Town and city offices, state agencies, and the clerks, administrators and finance staff who hold a lot of process in their heads. Permits, licensing, records requests, grant reporting, procurement paperwork.

Schools, districts and colleges

Business offices, athletic departments and administrators from K–12 through higher ed. Purchasing, restricted-fund reporting, enrollment paperwork, film and performance analysis.

Small business and non-profits

Operations that run on one or two people’s memory and no IT department. Property managers, service businesses, boards and volunteers doing by hand what the big version of them automated a decade ago.

Builders and the trades

General contractors putting up houses. Estimates, change orders, subcontractor scheduling, the photographs and paperwork that pile up on every job and get sorted on a Sunday.

Makers and creative work

Software developers and studios, fashion designers, artists and independent brands. Design iteration, production, listings and the dull half of a launch that eats the half you are good at.

Coaches and programs

Youth, club and school coaching staff, mostly volunteers. Film breakdown, opponent scouting, and the Sunday evening it costs to do by hand. The athletics detail.

Three things we believe that most firms do not

Your data is the advantage

If a platform learns from your operation and sells that improvement to everyone in your category, you have funded your competitor's capability. We build so what you know stays yours.

We design to what you have

There are a lot of these AI systems now, and they are not all alike. We pick the one that suits the job and your budget — including, very often, the one you already pay for and barely use. We will not send you out to buy what everyone else bought.

Leaving should be easy

What we build runs on your accounts, in your storage, documented well enough that you could replace us. Anything else is a trap with a nicer name.

Start by telling us about one job

Ten minutes, online, in your own words. We ask the same questions we would ask sitting in your office — all of them about how the work gets done, none of them about technology. At the end you get the job written down properly, start to finish. That is yours to keep whether you ever call us or not.

No name, no account, and nobody rings you unless you ask. Send it to us and we will tell you where we think the hours actually are — including when the honest answer is that there are not enough of them to bother.
