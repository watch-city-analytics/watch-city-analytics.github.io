We measure before we build, and we tell you when not to.

One workflow at a time. The assessment costs nothing. The work that follows should pay for itself out of the time it gives back.

Why the assessment is free

Because you cannot evaluate an AI proposal from the outside. You are asked to believe a claim about work you understand better than the person making it, priced in a way you cannot check. The reasonable response is to say no.

So we take the risk instead. We measure what the process costs you now, show you what changes on your own data, and put a number on it. If the number is not big enough to pay for the work, we say so and you owe nothing. That happens, and we would rather it happened before you paid us than after.

The same five steps, every time

This is the method the practice was built around. It is Sanjay Patel’s, worked out across thirty years of watching organizations buy the system first and discover the need afterward. Whoever is assigned to your job works this way, because it is the condition of working here.

i

We learn the process as it really runs

Including the exceptions and workarounds that never made it into the documentation, and the step everyone has stopped noticing because it has always been that way. You can start online in ten minutes, or we come and watch the work being done.

ii

We measure what it costs today

How long it takes, how many people touch it, where it waits. Most organizations have never measured this, and the number alone changes conversations. It is also what every later claim gets checked against.

iii

We show you the change on your own data

Your records, your film, your correspondence. Not a demo built on somebody else's example. Small is fine — the point is that you can judge it rather than take our word.

iv

We price against the time returned

Fixed scope, fixed fee, sized so the hours we give back cover the cost. We will tell you the year-three running cost before you commit, including model usage, because software that is affordable until it is not has burned enough people already.

v

We build it to be left behind

Running on your accounts, in your storage, documented well enough that your team operates it without us. We are happy to stay. You should never need us to.

Who actually does it

Two layers, and the combination is the point. Sanjay Patel is the horizontal one. Thirty years inside large technology companies, building systems and taking products to market, is useful here for exactly two reasons: it is long enough to know what a working method looks like, and long enough to know who is actually good at their trade. The bench is the vertical one — senior specialists with decades inside a single world, chosen on that judgment rather than from a directory.

When a job needs depth he does not have, he brings in the person who does, tells you their name, and stays accountable for the result. Neither layer alone produces as good an answer as the two together.

We bring the organic intelligence to the artificial kind.

The machine supplies speed and patience, and will read ten thousand documents without complaining. What it cannot supply is the instinct a clerk has about the one permit that is going to come back. That part is grown, not installed, and it already exists inside your organization. Our job is to get it into the system rather than leave it stranded in somebody’s head.

Engagements run fractional. A named senior specialist for the fraction of time the work genuinely takes — a few hours a week, over a few weeks to a few months. You get the expertise an enterprise budget buys, sized to your problem instead of to a payroll, and the pace that comes of pairing that expertise with people who are native to these tools. Nobody is handed your job, and nobody learns your business on your money.

The work we take on

The common thread is a process that a capable team is doing by hand because nobody has had time to look at it properly.

Operations and back office

The recurring documents, the reconciliation, the correspondence that follows a pattern. Work that is too varied for a template and too repetitive to deserve a person's afternoon.

Data you already have, unused

Forty years of records that live in filing cabinets, spreadsheets and somebody's head. Getting that into a form your organization can actually ask questions of is often the highest-value thing available.

Video and film analysis

Capture through breakdown, built on tools you control rather than a subscription that holds your archive. How this works for athletic programs.

Getting more from what you pay for

Most organizations own far more capability than they use — a district with Google in every classroom, a business whose cloud subscription does nothing but email. We start on the invoice before we recommend buying anything.

Data architecture and exit planning

Where your data lives, what it costs to keep, and what it would take to move it. This is our founder’s original discipline, and it is the question most organizations get to too late.

Teaching your team to carry it

Working sessions with the people who will actually use what we build, in their process, with their examples. Not a generic AI training course.
